The standings at the top of the table are always watched closely, but the medal counts for other countries are often just as valued. For example, Albania (2 medals), Cabo Verde, Dominica, and Saint Lucia (2 medals) won their nations' first Olympic medals at the Paris games. Dominica and Saint Lucia also each earned a Gold medal at these games. More than 60 countries have still yet to win an Olympic medal.

Predictions of the final medal counts are commonly made, but typically not based on historical medal counts but closer to the start of an upcoming Olympic games when current athletes scheduled to compete are known (for example: https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2024/virtual-medal-table-forecast/).

Data is provided of medal tables for all summer Olympic games, host countries, as well as the number of Olympic events at each games broken down by sport for all summer Olympic games through 2024. Use the data to:

- Develop a model for medal counts for each country (for Gold and total medals at a minimum). Use your model to forecast the medal table for the 2028 Los Angeles summer Olympics. What range of medals can we expect a country to win with reasonable confidence? What is the probability each country will win at least one medal in 2028? Are there any countries more likely to win their first medal (or first Gold medal)?
- A common observation is that host countries win more medals than usual during the Olympics they host. Do you find evidence for a host country effect? What other factors, if any, seem to matter?
- For an individual country, which disciplines or events are they most likely to earn a medal in? What factors suggest which medals may be won?
- How could your model be used to help the national Olympic committees (NOC) of individual countries improve their standing? For a given country, how might they invest resources to have the greatest impact on their total medal count?
- Produce a report of no more than 25 pages total that summarizes your results. Include a one-page memorandum to the International Olympic Committee (IOC) advising them on your recommendations and their potential impact.

## Glossary

- **Discipline:** A branch of a sport that includes one or more events.
- **Event:** A competition within a discipline that results in a ranking and awards (e.g. medals).
- **Host Country Effect:** An increase in the number of medals won by the host country during the games they host.
- **National Olympic Committee (NOC):** The national governing body responsible for a country’s participation in the Olympic Games.
- **Sport:** An athletic activity governed by a single International Sports Federation (IF).
